Binom1Tooling in production

A hybrid aircraft that makes 3D mobility real.

Not an eVTOL and not a toy: a wing, proven aviation solutions and one new idea. Two electric motors ahead of the wing blow air over it for take-off and landing, then fold their propellers away in cruise. A lawn-sized strip is enough, a second independent power system is always on board, and the aircraft flies between cities on the main engine.

Blown wing
Lift coefficient 3.5–4.2 instead of 1.2: lift-off at 52 km/h, stall at 45 km/h, ground roll 36 m.
Second power system
If the main engine stops, about 10 km of level flight on the electric motors instead of 2.4 km of gliding.
Parking space, not a hangar
Quick-detachable wing: the aircraft fits a car parking space or a trailer box.
With NERINGA on board
Autonomy and a wider weather window today; in perspective, fully automatic flight from strip to strip.

Why a wing, not an eVTOL

Urban eVTOL
About 20 minutes of flight, while aviation rules keep about 15 minutes as a reserve. Hovering needs thrust of about 1.5× the weight.
Binom1
Up to 5 hours of flight. From about 60 km/h the wing carries the aircraft; thrust only has to overcome drag, a fifth to a quarter of the weight.

NERINGA · HoustonLow-level layer complete

A new-generation three-level flight controller.

NERINGA combines proven control algorithms with an AI agent that advises the pilot, helps the pilot or takes over altogether. It flies take-off, the mission, the return and the landing on STM32 boards; Houston is the ground station that talks to it. Both are Lithuanian end-to-end development, written in our team.

What the AI agent does

  1. AdvisesKeeps the full picture and prompts the pilot.
  2. HelpsTakes over the hardest phases: approach, flare, touchdown.
  3. ReplacesFlies the whole flight; the pilot supervises or works.

Why autonomy widens the weather window

A car drives in fog, rain and low cloud; a small aircraft does not. NERINGA is built to see for itself.

Cameras
Ground, obstacles, horizon, other aircraft.
Terrain-following radar
The same, in fog and cloud.
Magnetic navigation
A rough position from the Earth's magnetic field, so visual navigation matches images only within that area, not the whole map. Published industry flight tests of magnetic navigation have shown accuracy within 550 m 64% of the time, and within 3.7 km at all times.

The goal: you sit in the aircraft and work on a client presentation while it flies itself.

Questions and answers

What is Binom1?

A single-seat hybrid light aircraft from Lithuania for everyday personal air travel. A petrol engine drives the nose propeller; two electric motors on a short front wing blow air over the main wing for take-off and landing, then fold their propellers away in cruise.

How much runway does Binom1 need?

About 40–60 m for take-off and landing: a mown grass strip of 100–150 m, the size of a village lawn, is enough. Stall speed is 45 km/h.

How fast and how far does it fly?

Cruise 170 km/h, maximum 220 km/h, range about 600 km or 5 hours (calculated). From an airpark 110 km out, Vilnius is about 45 minutes away.

Why a wing and not an eVTOL?

An urban eVTOL flies about 20 minutes, and aviation rules keep about 15 minutes in reserve. Binom1 flies up to 5 hours because the wing carries it; thrust only has to overcome drag.

What happens if the engine stops?

The electric motors are a second, independent power system: about 10 km of level flight instead of 2.4 km of gliding.

What is NERINGA?

Our own three-level flight controller: control algorithms plus an AI agent that advises the pilot, helps in the hardest phases or flies the whole flight. It is tested on the AD-27 UAV; Houston is its ground station.

What is AD-27 used for?

Long-endurance monitoring of forests and wildfire, agriculture, power lines, railways and search and rescue. It launches from a catapult or from wheels; 21 test flights in 2026.

When can I fly one, and can I invest?

Binom1 tooling is in production and the prototype is the next step. For partnerships, pilot projects or the investor deck, write to avmpilot@gmail.com.
